# Learner Review

Use learner review to find teaching defects that package checks cannot see. Unless real students actually participated, call this a structured virtual-reader review, not a survey, interview, focus group, or experiment.

## Select relevant perspectives

Choose perspectives that create genuinely different failure modes. A useful pool is:

- complete beginner
- non-specialist in the course domain
- technically advanced learner
- learner focused on employment or practice
- learner less fluent in Korean
- learner who checks numbers and sources carefully
- learner skeptical of the claimed benefits
- learner who learns through cases
- learner viewing in short mobile sessions
- instructor checking read-aloud rhythm
- learner responsible for law, safety, or governance

For an ordinary localized revision, one or two relevant perspectives plus the instructor read-aloud pass may be sufficient. Use five to ten perspectives for a new course, broad rewrite, or high-stakes release. Do not force the same list on every course.

## Two-pass protocol

### R1: first encounter

Read the entire deck and notes in order. Record:

- a term or actor appearing before explanation
- an unexplained change in time, scale, lens, or unit of analysis
- conclusion delayed behind dates, methods, or tool names
- screen/note mismatch
- a chart whose axes, denominator, or meaning remain unclear
- a legal or statistical condition likely to be generalized incorrectly
- excessive repetition, translationese, defensive prose, or read-aloud burden
- an example that is vivid but does not advance the judgment
- a final slide that lists concepts without enabling transfer

### R2: contextual reread

Present the slide with the immediately preceding and following context. Keep only problems that remain after this context is available.

Distinguish:

- **memory gap:** the learner cannot recall a term that was taught adequately
- **teaching gap:** the term or inference was never made understandable
- **misconception:** the deck actively supports a wrong interpretation

Revise teaching gaps and misconceptions. Do not overload the deck merely to eliminate every memory gap.

## Reporting

For each confirmed issue, report:

| Severity | Slide | Perspective | Learner interpretation | Evidence in screen/notes | Minimal fix |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|

Use these severities consistently:

- **P0:** unusable or dangerous output, missing required content, or a major factual/legal error
- **P1:** material learner misunderstanding, lost user edit, major visual obstruction, or failed synchronization
- **P2:** real but localized clarity, wording, contrast, alignment, provenance, or reproducibility defect

An exact fix is useful, but the learner interpretation is the reason for the fix.

## Common cross-slide checks

- the opening promise is answered
- each technical term is explained before it carries argumentative weight
- classifications are preceded by their dimensions
- cases are connected to the rule they illustrate
- the same framework name retains the same meaning
- a later summary matches the earlier method
- deletion or insertion does not leave dangling page references
- the final application uses concepts actually taught
- transitions vary naturally and give a reason for moving

## Saturation

When saturation is required, freeze the candidate before review. Record start and end hashes. If any issue is found and fixed, reset all clean-round counts. A clean learner review is one independent layer; it does not replace factual, visual, or package review.
